micromouse 4wd · a hypothetical

The 120 mm robot at Formula 1 speeds

Take the same aero model — real CAD frontal area, the drag equation, the measured wing — and wind it up to F1 pace: 360 km/h on the straight, 270 km/h through a fast corner. Every conclusion from the normal study inverts. Aero stops being negligible and starts dominating; the useless wing becomes a monster; and the maze corner turns out to be the one thing physics simply forbids.

1. On the straight, air becomes the dominant force

Drag scales with the square of speed, so going from 5 m/s to 100 m/s multiplies it by 400. What was an ignorable 29 mN becomes 11.5 N — more than five times the robot's weight, pushing back with 5.3 g of deceleration and demanding 1.15 kW just to hold speed.

Two regime changes the low-speed model quietly assumed away: Reynolds number climbs to 8e+05 (genuinely turbulent, ~20× the micromouse's own), so the boundary layer and wake are now fully turbulent; and Mach number reaches 0.29 — past ~0.3 air can no longer be treated as incompressible, and even here a Prandtl-Glauert correction adds ~5% to Cd. The lattice-Boltzmann pictures in the main aero study are still topologically right (sharp-edged separation barely moves with Re) but the absolute forces would need a compressible solver.

2. The wing that was useless is suddenly a monster

At micromouse pace a wing made fractions of a milliNewton and we dismissed it. Downforce also scales with v², and at 270 km/h the same surfaces come alive:

An F1-grade underbody would press this 220 g robot into the floor with over 131 N — 61 times its weight. At low speed the required lift coefficient to matter was an absurd CL 196; at F1 speed a perfectly ordinary CL 3 does it. This is the whole reason downforce is an F1 obsession and a non-topic for small slow robots: it is a high-speed phenomenon, full stop.

3. ...but the corner is physically impossible

Here the hypothetical breaks against geometry. A micromouse corner is a 90 mm radius. Holding that radius at 75 m/s demands a centripetal acceleration of:

62,500 m/s² = 6,370 g
v²/R with v = 75 m/s, R = 0.09 m

No tyre, no downforce, no material survives thousands of g. Even giving the robot that monster F1 underbody — 61× its weight in grip — the fastest it could round a 90 mm corner is about 11.2 m/s. To actually take a corner at 75 m/s you would need a radius of 249 m (~1385 maze cells) — which is exactly why F1 corners are tens of metres wide, not 9 centimetres.

The real lesson of the whole aero series, in one line: aerodynamic force follows v² and needs SPEED to exist; cornering grip needs speed but the geometry needs SPACE. A micromouse has neither — so aero is irrelevant to it. An F1 car has both — so aero defines it. Same equations, opposite worlds; this robot can borrow F1's straight-line drag but never its corners.
