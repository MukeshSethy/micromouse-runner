What the physics does

Four wheels, each with contact velocity from the body state (u, v, r). Slip is the difference between how fast the tyre is rolling and how fast it is travelling. Force follows F = μN along the slip direction, ramped over a 60 mm/s reference then saturated — so a tyre can never produce more than its circle allows.

Why it slides in turns

Both wheels on a side are locked together by the gear train, so a turn must scrub — the front and rear tyres on a side cannot follow different arcs. That scrub eats grip that the turn needs, which is why the limit here is lower than the textbook v = √(μgR).

Why the IMU is not optional

Toggle IMU gyro off and the controller falls back to encoder odometry for heading — real wheel rotations, but blind to contact-patch slip. Skid-steer scrubs in every turn, so the estimate walks off a few degrees per corner and the mouse fails within a handful of cells. That drift readout is the BNO055's whole job.

Where it breaks

Around 1 m/s the tyres start to give up in the tighter corners; by 1.5 m/s it runs wide and clips walls. Raise μ (silicone tyres) and the same corner comes back. That trade is the whole reason micromouse builders obsess over tyre compound.
